## The 2-Sentence Description
Lab Atlas maps every research lab at a university (what each studies, whether it works at the bench or the keyboard, and who it works with), built from publications, grants and directories with zero faculty effort. Universities license it so students write to the right labs and offices can see, and show, who gets in. *Example: at Emory it maps 7,078 investigators, of whom our model places only ~810 on the bench side.*

## Strongest Element
**Insight** (backed by a working product): the labs students want are a small, hidden fraction of the directory, and posting tools can't reveal them because faculty don't post. Traction is the weakest element (no revenue or signed pilot yet).

## Pitch Ordering
What You Do -> **Insight** -> Problem -> Solution (live demo scene) -> Traction -> Market -> Business Model -> Competition -> Team -> Ask. Insight leads because it's the most impressive element before revenue exists.

# Phase 6: Validation Plan -- Lab Atlas

> Session: `lab-atlas` | Generated: 2026-09-30 | Skill: `/sk-validate`
> Inputs: `05-offer.md` (Every Lab in 45 Days -- Founding Partner Program; $15K list mid-tier; Emory design-partner deal), `04-positioning.md`, `03-competitors.md`.
> **Recap:** Gold niche = undergraduate research offices at medical-center universities. Promise = "More students in the right labs, zero faculty effort." The main unknown is not *whether the product works* but **whether offices will pay for discovery when they already pay for a posting tool in a budget freeze.** Everything below is designed to answer that fast.

---

## Step 1: Discovery Call Frames

| Audience | Frame | Why |
|----------|-------|-----|
| **Emory Pathways Center / URP** | **Sales call (design partner)** | You have the relationship and a working product. The ask is a signed LOI with metrics, not more feedback. |
| **UR directors at peer institutions** | **Market Research -> Free Coaching** | Start with the Mom Test (their past behavior, their tools, their numbers). Then offer a free *preview atlas of their campus*. That's the Free Coaching equivalent: real value given, feedback received. |
| **Faculty (supply side)** | Market Research | Learn how they handle student emails today and whether a one-click "taking undergrads?" email would be answered. |
| **Students (users)** | Market Research + usability | Watch them find a lab with and without Lab Atlas. |

**Progression:** Market Research (Oct-Nov) -> Free preview atlas (Nov-Jan) -> Sales/founding-partner offer (Jan-Mar, before FY2028 budget decisions).

### Board Counsel (Customer & Validation)
> "Our final conclusions are always based on cost per customer." -- Claude Hopkins, Customer & Validation Board (via `/sk-validate`)

> Chuck Feeney's partners "started distribution first," proving demand before inventory. -- Customer & Validation Board (via `/sk-validate`)

**The Board's view:** you have over-invested in product (v85!) relative to distribution. Stop adding features and **prove the channel**: can you get 10 UR directors on a call, and will 3 name a price? Track what each signed institution costs you to win (calls, travel, preview builds). That number decides whether this is a business.

## Step 2: Outreach Scripts

### 2a. Ask your champion for introductions (send this week)
> Subject: Could you introduce us to 5 peers?
>
> Hi [Pathways contact], thank you again for backing Lab Atlas. The most useful thing for us right now would be 20-minute conversations with 5 undergraduate research directors at other universities, ideally ones with medical schools, to learn how they handle lab discovery today. We're not pitching; we're learning. Would you be willing to send a short intro to anyone you know through CUR? I've drafted a two-line intro you can forward. Thank you!

### 2b. Warm email to a peer UR director (after an intro)
> Subject: How do your students find labs? (20 min, no pitch)
>
> Hi Dr. [Name], [Pathways contact] suggested I reach out. We're Emory students who built a map of every research lab at Emory (7,078 investigators, each placed on a wet-bench-to-computational spectrum). We're trying to learn how offices like yours help students find labs, especially in medical-school departments. Could I ask you a few questions for 20 minutes? Happy to share what we've learned about Emory's research landscape in return.

### 2c. LinkedIn connection + follow-up sequence
- **Request:** "Hi [Name], I work on undergraduate research access (building a campus lab map at Emory). I'd value connecting with people who run UR offices."
- **Day 1:** "Thanks for connecting! Curious: what share of your students find their lab through cold email vs. a posting platform?"
- **Day 3-5:** share a one-page insight: *"Only ~11% of 7,078 Emory investigators are predicted wet-bench. Here's what that means for advising pre-health students."* (Only share Emory figures once the Pathways Center okays it.)
- **Day 7-10:** "Would a 20-minute call be useful? We could build a free preview of your campus's research landscape as a thank-you."

### 2d. Community post (CUR program-directors list / LinkedIn)
> **What share of your faculty actually run bench labs?** We classified 7,078 investigators at one research university by method (bench / hybrid / computational) from their publications and grants. About 11% came out bench-side, 64% computational or clinical, 23% too little text to call. For advising, that means a student who wants pipettes has far fewer real options than the directory suggests. How do you help students narrow the field? We'd love to compare notes.

### 2e. Content hooks (3-5)
1. **Counter-intuitive:** "Your faculty directory has 7,000 names. Maybe 800 run the bench labs your pre-meds are looking for."
2. **Mistake to avoid:** "The #1 mistake in a student's first email to a PI (and the 3-line fix)."
3. **Hot take:** "Posting platforms don't widen access unless faculty are required to post. The data says so." (cite ASEE 2026 carefully)
4. **How-to:** "Wet lab or dry lab? How to tell from a lab's last 10 paper titles."
5. **Story:** "How a first-year found a lab in 2 weeks without knowing anyone" (only after a real pilot story, with consent).

### 2f. Faculty test email (one-click verify, 20 Emory PIs)
> Subject: Is this right? (1 click)
>
> Dr. [Name], students search Emory's lab atlas to find research that fits them. Your lab is listed as **[setting: e.g., hybrid]** based on your recent papers and grants. **Is that right?** [Yes] [No, it's mostly bench] [No, it's mostly computational]. **Taking undergraduates this term?** [Yes] [No] [Maybe later]. No login needed; you can opt out anytime.

## Step 3: Interview Question Bank

### UR directors (buyers)
1. "Walk me through how a student found a lab in your office last semester, start to finish."
2. "What tools do you pay for today for research opportunities? When do they renew? Who decided?"
3. "What share of your faculty actually use [ForagerOne/Handshake/your tool]? How do you know?"
4. "What did you do the last time a student asked for 'a lab doing X'? How long did it take?"
5. "What numbers does your dean or provost ask you for? Which one worries you most?"
6. "Tell me about a tool you bought that faculty didn't adopt. What happened?"
7. "If you had $10K more next year, what would you spend it on?" (willingness-to-pay without pitching)
8. "Who else would have to say yes to a new tool: IT, procurement, a dean?"
9. "What's your budget cycle? When are requests due?"
10. After the preview demo only: "What would this be worth to you per year? What would make it a no?"

### Students
- "How did you find your current lab (or try to)? How many emails did you send? How many replies?"
- "What did you wish you knew about a lab before emailing?"

### Faculty
- "How many unsolicited student emails do you get per term? How many are well-targeted?"
- "Would you answer a one-click 'taking undergrads?' email each term?"

### Red flags (from `/sk-validate`)
- "Yeah, that's annoying," but they've never tried to solve it. (Low urgency.)
- "We'd definitely use it," but no number and no budget owner. (Politeness.)
- Changing the subject to postings and applications. (They want workflow, not discovery.)

### Green lights
- They already pay for a tool and complain about faculty participation. (Proven spend + pain.)
- They pull up their own participation or equity numbers unprompted.
- They ask "Could you do this for our campus?" before you pitch.

## Step 5: MVP Spec (for commercialization, not the product)
You already have the product MVP. What's unproven is **repeatability**. Two MVPs:

1. **"Atlas #2 in 14 days."** Build a private preview atlas for **one non-Emory medical-center university** using only open data (OpenAlex/ORCID for publications, NIH RePORTER + NSF awards, the public faculty directory), with a hard cap of 14 days and a time log. Success = ≥90% of research-active faculty covered, wet/dry classified with the existing model, and total human effort ≤40 hours. This is the single most important test of "mass" commercialization.
2. **"Emory pilot kit."** The smallest version an office can run with: hosted behind Emory login (or password-protected), faculty one-click verify email, a placement form, deep links to ForagerOne profiles, and a one-page advisor guide.

**First 3 people to show them (write the names):** (1) the Emory College URP director, (2) a Pathways Center advisor who builds lab lists for students, (3) a GDBBS program administrator. **[Founder to name]**

### Kill / pivot criteria
- Emory won't sign even a $0 LOI with metrics by Dec 2026 -> **your home market doesn't value it enough**; pivot to the Silver niche (graduate programs) or a data-licensing partnership.
- Fewer than 3 of 10 external directors name a price -> discovery isn't a budget line; sell **to or through ForagerOne** (license the classifier and pipeline) instead of competing.
- Atlas #2 takes >40 hours of human effort -> the pipeline isn't ready for "mass". Fix automation before selling beyond 3 institutions.

## Red Flags
- **Distribution is Weak.** Without Pathways Center introductions, the external call target is at risk.
- Pilot data about students (placements, first-gen status) is sensitive. Get the FERPA handling and IRB determination right, or the pilot can't be reported.

## Yellow Flags
- Emory figures (11% wet-bench, etc.) should not be published without Pathways Center or Emory Communications agreement.
- A comparison cohort may be hard to construct fairly. Agree on the design in the LOI.

# Raw: Competitor Profiles (Wave 1 / A1)
*Skill: sk-competitors | Research date: 2026-09-30 | Depth: Standard (run sequentially, no sub-agents)*

## 1. ForagerOne (direct, specialist leader)
- **What:** University-wide marketplace connecting students and faculty for research and creative activity. Features: faculty profiles (auto-created from university web pages or profile systems, then claimed and edited by faculty), an "Accepting Students" toggle, opportunity postings and applications, student profiles, bookmarks, a messenger that routes to university email, SSO, and analytics for administrators. Separate **Symposium** product for virtual/hybrid research symposia. [Data: Emory, Mizzou, UK, Wayne State pages]
- **Founded:** ~2017-18 by JHU undergraduates Ansh Bhammar and Yash Jain (co-CEOs); Nitin Kumar (CTO), Michael Ashmead (CPO). JHU campus announcements date from Feb 2018. [Data]
- **Origin:** A Google Sheet of ~100-200 scraped life-science faculty profiles, then an unsanctioned student-built platform with grassroots adoption, then institutional sales. Symposium was built in a one-week hackathon in April 2020; about 50 institutions used it for virtual events. [Data: Coach Carter substack, 2021]
- **Funding:** MassChallenge accelerator; 2020 Bisciotti Foundation Prize ($30K, first place); "consciously avoided raising dilutive capital" as of Mar 2021. No later rounds found. [Data, 2021 -- potentially outdated]
- **Scale:** "Empowering over 200 institutions" (2024 NCUR listing). [Data, 2024] Named customers: Johns Hopkins, **Emory (College URP, Pathways Center)**, UMD, Baylor (2025), Wayne State (Feb 2025), Kentucky, Hawaiʻi Mānoa (2023), Drexel, Cornell, Brandeis, Spelman, Mizzou, Grand Valley State, High Point, Eastern Michigan.
- **Team size:** DATA GAP (LinkedIn page exists; headcount not retrieved).
- **Moat type:** switching costs (claimed faculty profiles, SSO, office workflows) + brand/trust inside the undergraduate-research community. Durability: 3-5 years.
- **Key vulnerability:** coverage and freshness depend on faculty behavior (claiming profiles, flipping the toggle, posting). ASEE 2026 evidence: voluntary faculty use of a posting platform produced **no** participation increase. [Data]
- **Threat:** **High** (same buyer, same budget line, entrenched at Emory).

## 2. Handshake (adjacent, career platform used for research postings)
- **What:** Career network where students find jobs and internships. Many UR offices route research postings through it (UC Davis URC, KU CURF, Arizona, UNT, Kennesaw State, Virginia Tech). [Data]
- **Evidence:** In a Virginia Tech pilot, research postings received 10-20 applications within about a week. [Data]
- **Moat:** network effects (students and employers) + near-universal career-center adoption.
- **Key vulnerability:** only covers *posted* positions and treats research as a job. It can't show the landscape of labs that never post.
- **Threat:** **Medium** (the "good enough, already paid for" default).

## 3. PairMe (homegrown, local competitor)
- **What:** Georgia Tech platform created by BME student Amy Liu. Mentors (faculty, grad students, postdocs, research scientists) post openings; students apply with GT credentials. Began in the Coulter BME department (joint GT/Emory) and expanded to all GT majors. [Data]
- **Relevance:** directly serves the **Emory BME** population in Atlanta.
- **Moat:** none beyond a campus relationship. **Threat:** **Low-Medium** (local only).

## 4. Institution-built posting platforms (homegrown category)
- Notre Dame's research-for-credit platform (ASEE 2026): faculty post, students apply, structured review. Mandated in Engineering (+57.5% students doing research for credit), voluntary in Science (no increase). [Data]
- UConnect (University of Miami, peer networking); ConnectEd Research (UCLA student organization). [Data]
- **Threat:** **Medium** as a category, because LLM tooling makes internal builds cheaper each year. [Opinion]

## 5. Research-profile / research-networking systems (adjacent)
- **Elsevier Pure** (commercial RIMS: 250 implementations, 185K researcher profiles per a Wikipedia comparison page -- potentially outdated). **Symplectic Elements** (Digital Science). **Profiles RNS** (NIH-funded open source; Harvard Catalyst). **VIVO** (open source since 2003). [Data]
- **Job they do:** expertise finding, reporting and research networking for faculty and administrators.
- **Key vulnerability:** not student-facing; no undergraduate signals; costly to run.
- **Threat:** **Low-Medium** (and a potential *data source/partner*).

## 6. General-purpose AI assistants (substitute)
- ChatGPT (including Deep Research), Claude and Gemini can browse faculty pages and suggest labs. [Knowledge-based -- verify independently]
- **Key vulnerability:** no institutional ground truth (who takes undergrads), incomplete coverage, possible hallucination, no accountability.
- **Threat:** **Medium** now, rising.

## 7. Status quo (the real incumbent)
- Faculty web pages, cold emails, advisor-curated lists, word of mouth, lab-mates. Free and familiar.
- Evidence of inequity: an audit study emailed 6,548 professors at 259 institutions as fictional prospective PhD students; faculty responded more often to white male names. [Data; PhD-applicant context, not undergrads]
- Student voice: "Given the sheer number of STEM students and a lack of an official application process, getting into labs can be competitive and time-consuming" (Daily Bruin, 2023). [Data]
- **Threat:** **High** (inertia).

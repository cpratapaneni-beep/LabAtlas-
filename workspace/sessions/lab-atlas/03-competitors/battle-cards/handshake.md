# Battle Card: Handshake (research postings)
*Last updated: 2026-09-30 | Threat: MEDIUM*

## At a Glance
- **What they do:** Career network; many UR offices tell faculty to post research positions there (UC Davis, KU, Arizona, UNT, Kennesaw State, Virginia Tech). [Data]
- **Founded:** 2014 [Knowledge-based] | **Funding:** venture-scale [Knowledge-based] | **Team:** large
- **Price to UR office:** usually $0 marginal (the career center holds the license) [Assumption] | **Model:** institution license + employer products
- **Best for:** labs that *do* post paid research-assistant roles.

## Their Strengths (be honest)
- Students already live there; alerts, profiles and applications are built in. [Data]
- VT pilot: 10-20 applications per research posting within about a week. [Data]
- Zero procurement for the UR office.

## Their Weaknesses (your openings)
- Only covers **posted** positions. Most labs never post; they answer emails.
- Research is a **job** in Handshake's model; there's no lab landscape, methods, publications or collaborators.
- Volume without fit can swamp small labs. [Opinion]

## How to Win Against Them
- **When they say "Faculty can just post on Handshake,"** respond: "They can, and a few do. Lab Atlas shows the 90%+ who never will, and links to Handshake whenever a lab *has* posted."
- **Lead with:** "Handshake shows the jobs; Lab Atlas shows the labs."

## When They Win Over You
- Paid, formal RA roles with HR onboarding. Career-center-led institutions with no UR office budget.

## Their Customers' Top Complaint
DATA GAP for the research use case. Recommend asking UR directors which share of their research placements came through Handshake.

## Key Vulnerability
Posting-only coverage. It can't answer "who works on X here?"

## Churn Signals
- Not applicable (UR offices don't buy it; they borrow it).

## Watch For
- AI-driven "research opportunities" features inside Handshake would put a free, well-distributed competitor in front of students. [Opinion]

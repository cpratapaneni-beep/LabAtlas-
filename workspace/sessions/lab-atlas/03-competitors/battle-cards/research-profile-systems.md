# Battle Card: Research-Profile Systems (Elsevier Pure, Symplectic Elements, Profiles RNS, VIVO)
*Last updated: 2026-09-30 | Threat: LOW-MEDIUM (also a potential data source)*

## At a Glance
- **What they do:** Research-information management and expertise search. Faculty profiles built from publications, grants and HR data.
- **Scale:** Pure had 250 implementations and 185K profiles (per a comparison page -- potentially outdated). Profiles RNS is NIH-funded open source (Harvard Catalyst). VIVO is open source (2003). [Data]
- **Price:** Pure and Elements are enterprise (not public; often six figures) [Knowledge-based]; Profiles RNS and VIVO software is free.
- **Best for:** research offices, libraries and CTSA hubs (faculty-to-faculty expertise finding, reporting).

## Their Strengths (be honest)
- Deep, curated publication and grant data; institutional system-of-record status.
- Co-author networks (Profiles RNS) that rival Lab Atlas's graph.

## Their Weaknesses (your openings)
- Built for faculty and administrators, not **students**: no "takes undergrads", no methods classification, no outreach help.
- Expensive (commercial) or staff-heavy (open source) to maintain.

## How to Win Against Them
- **When they say "We already have an expert-finder,"** respond: "Great, we'll ingest it. Lab Atlas turns your research-profile data into a student-facing atlas with methods, availability and outreach coaching. We sit on top; we don't replace it."
- **Lead with:** student outcomes, and integration (use their data, lower our onboarding cost).

## When They Win Over You
- The buyer is the research office, the job is faculty-to-faculty expertise search, and the existing contract covers it.

## Their Customers' Top Complaint
DATA GAP (not mined).

## Key Vulnerability
Not designed for undergraduates.

## Churn Signals
- Budget cuts to library and research-office systems. [Opinion]

## Watch For
- Elsevier or Digital Science adding student-facing "research opportunity" portals. [Opinion]

# Battle Card: General AI Assistants (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini with web search)
*Last updated: 2026-09-30 | Threat: MEDIUM, rising*

## At a Glance
- **What they do:** Answer "which labs at my university study X?" by searching faculty pages and papers. [Knowledge-based -- verify independently]
- **Price:** free to ~$20/month for students; institution-wide education licenses exist. [Knowledge-based]
- **Best for:** a student's first exploratory question.

## Their Strengths (be honest)
- Zero setup, conversational, cross-institution, improving monthly.
- Draft outreach emails instantly.

## Their Weaknesses (your openings)
- No institutional ground truth: they don't know who takes undergrads, who is emeritus, or which "Dr. Lee" wrote which paper.
- Incomplete coverage and occasional hallucination; no calibrated confidence.
- Unlimited cold-email generation *increases* faculty inbox load, the opposite of what offices want. [Opinion]

## How to Win Against Them
- **When they say "Students can just ask ChatGPT,"** respond: "They can, and they will get plausible lists. Lab Atlas gives the complete, verified list for your campus: every investigator, whether they work at the bench, their current NIH funding, and (with placements) whether they've taken undergrads recently. With your office's guidance on how to reach out."
- **Lead with:** completeness, verification, institutional stewardship.

## When They Win Over You
- A student who wants a quick idea list and will verify it themselves.

## Their Customers' Top Complaint
Hallucinated or outdated faculty information. [Knowledge-based]

## Key Vulnerability
No verified, institution-specific data.

## Churn Signals
- N/A.

## Watch For
- Universities connecting AI assistants to their own faculty data (retrieval over directories). That's a make-vs-buy threat, and also an integration opportunity (license the Lab Atlas dataset as the retrieval source). [Opinion]

# Battle Card: Status Quo (faculty web pages + cold email + advisor lists)
*Last updated: 2026-09-30 | Threat: HIGH (inertia)*

## At a Glance
- **What it is:** Students browse department pages, ask peers, get a list from an advisor, and cold-email 10-50 professors.
- **Price:** "free", paid for in student time, advisor time, faculty inbox noise and inequity.

## Its Strengths (be honest)
- Familiar, flexible, and it works for well-networked students.
- Advisors add judgment no tool has.

## Its Weaknesses (your openings)
- **Inequitable:** faculty responded more to white male names in an audit of 6,548 professors (PhD-applicant emails). [Data]
- **Wasteful:** at Emory only ~11% of listed investigators are predicted wet-bench [Data, v85], so blind emailing mostly misses.
- **Informal and network-dependent** ("hidden door"). [Data, ASEE 2026]

## How to Win Against It
- **When they say "Our students figure it out,"** respond: "Which ones? Your first-gen students are the ones the cold-email system filters out. Lab Atlas makes the hidden curriculum visible for everyone."
- **Lead with:** equity and advisor time saved.

## When It Wins Over You
- Small colleges where students can walk the halls; offices with no budget and no mandate.

## Its Users' Top Complaint
"Given the sheer number of STEM students and a lack of an official application process, getting into labs can be competitive and time-consuming." -- Daily Bruin, 2023 [Data]

## Key Vulnerability
Equity outcomes can be measured, and they are bad.

## Churn Signals
- New equity mandates, participation KPIs, provost attention.

## Watch For
- Budget freezes reinforce the status quo ("no new tools this year").

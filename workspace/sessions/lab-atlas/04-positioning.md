## Positioning (3-Sentence Summary)
Lab Atlas is a **lab discovery platform** that maps every investigator at a research university from publications, grants and directories, with no faculty data entry, and shows whether each lab works at the bench or the keyboard. It serves undergraduate research offices at **universities with academic medical centers**, where thousands of investigators are invisible to students. It sits **alongside** the posting tools offices already own (ForagerOne, Handshake), making them pay off instead of competing for the same budget line.

## Positioning Statement
**Moore:** For directors of undergraduate research at research universities with medical centers who need more students in the right labs without adding work for faculty, Lab Atlas is a lab discovery platform that maps every investigator on campus (what they study, whether they work at the bench or the keyboard, and who they work with) from publications, grants and directories. Unlike opportunity-posting platforms, which only show faculty who opt in, Lab Atlas covers every lab from the record and links straight to the posting tool you already use.

**Onliness (Neumeier):** Lab Atlas is the only lab discovery platform that maps every investigator on campus, and whether each lab works at the bench or the keyboard, without asking faculty to do anything. *(Passes: the method spectrum is unique; see the validation in `positioning-doc.md`.)*

## Competitive Alternatives
1. Cold email + department pages + advisor lists (status quo, the real incumbent)
2. ForagerOne (posting marketplace, already at Emory): **complement, don't replace**
3. Handshake postings: "Handshake shows the jobs; Lab Atlas shows the labs."
4. Homegrown tools (PairMe for GT/Emory BME)
5. AI assistants (students' first stop; no verified local truth)
6. Research-profile systems (faculty-facing; ingest them)

Full map: [competitive-alternatives.md](04-positioning/competitive-alternatives.md)

## Market Category
**"Lab discovery platform"**, a **subcategory** (big fish, small pond) within undergraduate-research tools. Chosen over "research opportunity platform" (a head-to-head workflow comparison you'd lose) and "research access intelligence" (category creation, no data yet). Grow into access analytics after the pilot.

## Value Themes
1. **See every lab.** Complete coverage from the record; no faculty effort.
2. **Know the lab before you write.** Measured bench-vs-keyboard setting, publications, funding, collaborators; fewer, better-matched emails.
3. *(Earned after the pilot)* **Fair access you can measure.** Placements and equity outcomes.

## Best-Fit Customer
An undergraduate research office at a research university **with an academic medical center**, serving a pre-health and life-science-heavy student body, measured on participation and equity, with a posting tool that suffers from low faculty participation (or no tool), and with director-level authority for sub-threshold purchases.

## Elevator Pitch (30 seconds)
Most students find a research lab by cold-emailing professors they found on department pages. It wastes their time, floods faculty inboxes, and favors students who already know how the system works. Lab Atlas builds a complete map of every investigator on your campus from publications, grants and directories, with no faculty data entry. It tells students what each lab studies, whether it works at the bench or at a keyboard, and who it works with. Students write to fewer, better-matched labs. Your office sees the whole field. And every profile links to the ForagerOne or Handshake workflow you already run. At Emory it maps 7,078 investigators today.

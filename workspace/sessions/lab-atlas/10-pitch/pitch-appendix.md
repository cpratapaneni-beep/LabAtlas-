# Pitch Appendix -- Q&A Preparation: Lab Atlas
*Skill: sk-pitch | Generated: 2026-09-30*

## Top 10 likely questions (with prepared answers)

**1. "ForagerOne is already at Emory. Why would Emory pay for you too?"**
"Because ForagerOne shows students the faculty who participate, and Lab Atlas shows them every lab, with evidence, and sends them to ForagerOne to make contact. We'll prove it in a Spring pilot with agreed metrics. If outcomes don't move, Emory shouldn't renew." *(Don't claim Emory has agreed to pay until it has.)*

**2. "What stops ForagerOne from building this?"**
"Nothing permanent. So we're building what's hard to copy: a placement record nobody else collects, a pipeline that onboards a campus in 14 days, and relationships. We'd also welcome a partnership. If they'd rather license our classifier and pipeline, that's a good outcome."

**3. "Isn't this just a directory anyone can build with ChatGPT?"**
"The website, yes. The hard parts are merging nine messy sources into one record per person, attaching the right papers despite namesakes, classifying methods at 91.6% held-out accuracy with calibrated confidence, and refreshing monthly. We publish our error rates; a chatbot can't tell you when it's wrong."

**4. "Who owns the IP, you or Emory?"**
"Under Emory Policy 7.6, students own their IP unless Emory funding, substantial Emory support or an external funding agreement was involved. We've requested a written determination from OTT. If Emory has rights, we'll license them. Either way, Emory keeps a royalty-free educational license." *(Only say "requested" once it's true; see the roadmap.)*

**5. "The market looks small. Why invest?"**
"The US core is about $12M a year. That supports a capital-efficient company: $1M ARR is about 8% share. Upside comes from modules (graduate rotations, research development), English-speaking markets, and strategic acquirers who already sell to these offices. We're raising non-dilutive money first for exactly that reason."

**6. "How accurate is the wet/dry call, and what if it's wrong?"**
"91.6% overall on a held-out test; hybrids are our weakest call. Every profile shows confidence, records with too little text are left unclassified rather than guessed, and faculty can correct their label in one click. Corrections become new training data."

**7. "What does onboarding a new university take?"**
"A directory export (or the public directory), plus open publication and award data. Our target is a live atlas in 45 days, with the first 14 days to a working preview. We're running that test on a second university this winter."

**8. "What happens when you graduate?"**
"We've incorporated [or will by DATE], have founder vesting, and will offer customers data-export and continuity terms. [If applicable: founders' post-graduation plans.]"

**9. "Will faculty accept their profiles and emails being shown?"**
"All of it is from their public record; emails sit behind campus sign-in; any faculty member can correct or opt out in one click; and the product exists to *reduce* untargeted email. We'll recruit faculty advisors before launch."

**10. "What evidence says better discovery increases placements?"**
"Direct evidence is what our pilot will produce. Adjacent evidence: structured access raised research participation 57.5% when a platform was mandated (ASEE 2026), and faculty response bias in cold email is documented. We'll report our own numbers by May 2027."

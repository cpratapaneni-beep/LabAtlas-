# 5-Minute Pitch Narrative: Lab Atlas
*Skill: sk-pitch | Generated: 2026-09-30*

**Ordering rationale:** no revenue yet, so lead with **insight** (the 810-of-7,078 finding and a working product), then problem, solution (show, don't tell), traction, market, model, competition, team, ask.

---

### 1. What we do (0:00-0:30)
"Lab Atlas maps every research lab at a university: what it studies, whether it works at the bench or at a keyboard, and who it works with. It's built from publications, grants and directories, with zero faculty effort. For example, at Emory it maps 7,078 investigators across nine schools and institutes."
*Speaker note:* have the atlas open on the wet-to-dry axis behind you.

### 2. Insight (0:30-1:15)
"Mapping Emory taught us something non-obvious. Of 7,078 listed investigators, our classifier (91.6% accurate on a held-out test, with calibrated confidence on every call) places about 810 on the bench side, around 64% on the computational or clinical side, and 23% have too little public text to call at all. The labs undergrads usually mean by 'research' are a small, hidden fraction of the directory. Discovery is the bottleneck, not willingness."
*Transition:* "So how do students find those 810 today?"

### 3. Problem (1:15-2:00)
"Cold email. They scrape department pages and email dozens of professors. It's slow, it floods faculty inboxes, and it's unequal: in an audit of 6,548 professors, identical emails got more responses under white male names. Universities bought posting platforms to fix this, but posting depends on faculty. In one natural experiment, voluntary posting produced no participation gain; only a mandate did."

### 4. Solution: show, don't tell (2:00-2:45)
"A first-year opens Lab Atlas and types 'immune cells in tumors, hands-on bench work.' She sees 23 labs ranked by fit, each with its setting and confidence, recent papers, NIH funding and collaborators. She follows the graph from one PI to two collaborators she'd never have found. On each profile, one click goes to the lab's ForagerOne page. Faculty get fewer, better-targeted emails, and can correct their profile in one click." *(Numbers in this scene are illustrative; demo live.)*

### 5. Traction (2:45-3:15)
"We've built and iterated the full Emory atlas (v85). Emory's Pathways Center, the office that runs undergraduate research, supports it. Next: a Spring 2027 pilot with agreed metrics (placements, reply rates, first-gen gaps) and a 14-day test building a second university's atlas from open data."
*Speaker note:* don't overstate. There's no revenue and no signed pilot yet.

### 6. Market (3:15-3:40)
"Bottom-up: 187 R1s at $30K, 139 R2s at $15K, 216 research colleges at $8K. About $12 million a year in the US core, more with graduate-rotation and research-development modules. The first win is medical-center universities, where the directory is biggest and least legible."

### 7. Business model (3:40-4:00)
"An institutional license tiered by investigators covered: $6K, $15K or $30K a year. Onboarding is waived on two-year terms, and modules come on top. Gross margin is about 83% at list."

### 8. Competition (4:00-4:20)
"ForagerOne is the specialist leader, at 200+ schools and at Emory. They run the transaction; we do the discovery and link to them. If they'd rather license our classifier and pipeline than build them, that's a good outcome too."

### 10. The Ask (4:40-5:00)
"We're raising **$150K, non-dilutive first**, to reach **five paying universities, 14-day onboarding and published Emory pilot outcomes by June 30, 2027**. And we'd love introductions to undergraduate research directors at medical-center universities."

## Step 1-2: Structured niches (Person + Problem + Promise)

### N1 -- Undergraduate lab discovery for research universities with an academic medical center ★
- **Person:** Director of an Office of Undergraduate Research (or a Pathways-style center) at an R1/R2 university that has a medical school. Small team (1-5 staff), measured on participation and equity, often already paying for ForagerOne or using Handshake.
  - Three real people to name: the Emory College URP director (Pathways Center), plus two peers they know through CUR. **[Founder to name]**
- **Problem:** Students can't see which of thousands of investigators run labs that fit them and take undergraduates. At Emory only ~11% of 7,078 listed investigators are predicted wet-bench [Data, v85 model]. So students cold-email blindly (which is also inequitable), and posting-based tools stall because faculty won't volunteer. In one natural experiment, voluntary posting produced no participation gain while mandated use produced +57.5% [Data, ASEE 2026].
- **Promise:** *More students in the right labs, zero faculty effort.* (8 words)

### N2 -- Graduate rotation & recruitment atlas ★
- **Person:** Director or administrator of a biomedical PhD umbrella program (e.g., Emory GDBBS, 8 programs, 362 rostered faculty [Data]).
- **Problem:** First-years pick rotations from hundreds of faculty using stale "accepting students" lists. The v85 GDBBS snapshot has 0 of 362 faculty flagged as accepting [Data], which shows how weak the signal is.
- **Promise:** *Every rotation-ready lab, current, in one map.*

### N3 -- Discovery layer sold alongside ForagerOne / Handshake ★
- **Person:** UR offices already on ForagerOne (200+ institutions [Data, 2024 listing]); ForagerOne itself as a partner.
- **Problem:** A posting platform only covers faculty who opt in, and its profiles are shallow on methods.
- **Promise:** *Make every lab worth messaging, before anyone posts.*

### N4 -- Faculty collaboration / research-networking atlas ★
- **Person:** Research development office director; CTSA hub informatics lead.
- **Problem:** Faculty can't find collaborators across silos; research-profile systems are expensive or stale.
- **Promise:** *Find your next co-investigator in minutes.*

### N5 -- Research-landscape report for provosts and deans ★
- **Person:** Vice provost for research or strategy; deans.
- **Problem:** Leaders lack a current picture of the research enterprise (wet/dry mix, clusters, NIH exposure).
- **Promise:** *Your whole research enterprise, on one page.*

### N9 -- Academic-partner scouting for biotech/pharma BD (unstarred, scored for contrast)
- **Person:** External-innovation and BD managers at pharma and biotech companies.
- **Problem:** Finding the right academic lab or collaborator is slow.
- **Promise:** *The right academic lab in hours, not weeks.*

### Scoring rationale (short)
- **N1 "Pays $2K+" = Y:** budgets for this class of tool clearly exist (InfoReady's Gold package ran $24,500/yr at SFA State [Data]; ForagerOne is paid for at 200+ schools [Data]). But many target offices already spend that budget on ForagerOne, and 2025-26 federal cuts froze spending at Emory and elsewhere [Data]. **Painful = Y:** important, rarely urgent; equity mandates and participation KPIs make it moderately urgent. **Growing = Y:** undergraduate-research emphasis keeps rising, but university budgets are contracting. [Opinion]
- **N4 greens come from budget:** research offices spend more. But the space is crowded with Elsevier Pure (250 implementations), Symplectic Elements, and free Profiles RNS/VIVO [Data], and it pulls the team away from its student mission. [Opinion]
- **N2 "Growing" = R:** biomedical PhD intake is under pressure from NIH funding cuts in 2025-26. [Knowledge-based -- verify independently]
- **N3 "Pays" = R:** asking an office to pay twice for "student-faculty matching" in a budget freeze is the hardest sale in this list. As a *positioning posture* (complement, don't replace) it is valuable. As a *standalone business* it is weak.

### Low-status test
Every niche except N9 passes the "good for you, dear" test. A research directory for college advisors is unglamorous, which is good: nobody is racing a venture-backed rocket ship into it. [Opinion]

### Premium market check
Research universities with academic medical centers are the **9% premium tier**. They have the largest, most opaque research enterprises (Emory: 7,078 investigators, 87% in the School of Medicine [Data]), and that's where "zero faculty effort" coverage matters most. Small colleges (mass tier) have 50-300 faculty whom students can simply walk up to.

## Board Counsel (Market Focus)

> "How finding an affluent niche of passionate customers can be a better strategy than competing on price and volume." -- Joe Coulombe (Trader Joe's), Market Focus Board

- **Agreement:** Coulombe and Walton would both say don't fight ForagerOne across its 200+ institutions on its terms. Pick the segment it serves least well: huge medical-center research enterprises where posting coverage is thinnest and the wet/dry distinction matters most.
- **Disagreement:** Munger's circle-of-competence filter favors N1 (you have built it). A pure market-size lens favors N4/N9 (bigger budgets). **Resolution:** start in N1 and keep N4/N5 as the upsell path into the same accounts.
- **Warning** (Van Doren, from `/sk-niche`): *"Why were we depending on a handful of buyers anyway?"* Emory cannot be your only customer for long. Customer #2 matters more than customer #1.

## Red Flags
- **The Gold buyer at your own campus already pays a competitor.** Emory College's Undergraduate Research Programs, inside the Pathways Center, runs ForagerOne [Data]. "Support" is not "budget" until a document with a number exists.
- **Coverage mismatch:** only 112 Emory College STEM investigators are in v85, versus 6,136 in the School of Medicine [Data]. The Gold buyer's own faculty (humanities and social sciences included) are mostly missing.

## Yellow Flags
- Budget contraction across higher ed (hiring freezes and operating cuts at Emory in 2025 [Data]) may make "Pays $2K+" slip from Y to R for new-line-item purchases.
- Silver (N2) depends on biomedical PhD cohort sizes, which are under pressure. [Knowledge-based]

# Messaging Implications: Lab Atlas
*Skill: sk-positioning | Generated: 2026-09-30*

## Messaging Hierarchy
1. **See every lab.** Every investigator in scope, built from the record, no faculty effort.
2. **Know the lab before you write.** Bench or keyboard, publications, funding, collaborators.
3. **Works with what you have.** Links to ForagerOne/Handshake; ingests your profile system.
4. **Honest by design.** Every figure carries its confidence and limits (builds trust with faculty and IT reviewers).
5. *(After the pilot)* **Fair access you can measure.** Placements and equity outcomes.

## Category Label
**"lab discovery platform"**. Use this exact phrase in every channel.

## Value Anchor
Compare value to **advisor hours and student placements**, not to software:
- "An advisor building a hand-curated lab list for one student spends [X] minutes; Lab Atlas does it for every student." **[Founder to measure X at Emory]**
- Secondary anchor: the cost of the posting platform already owned. "Makes it pay off."

## Customer Language vs. Category Language
| Customer verb/noun (use) | Category/vendor noun (avoid in headlines) |
|--------------------------|-------------------------------------------|
| find a lab | research opportunity |
| write to the right lab | outreach optimization |
| bench or computer / wet or dry | research modality |
| takes undergraduates | availability signal |
| who works on X here | expertise discovery |
| every lab | comprehensive coverage |

## Words to Use / Avoid
| Use | Avoid | Why |
|-----|-------|-----|
| lab, investigator, find, fit, every, evidence, bench, computational | "AI-powered", "matching algorithm" | AI hype triggers HECVAT scrutiny and faculty distrust; "algorithm" implies replacing faculty judgment |
| links to your ForagerOne / Handshake | "replace ForagerOne", "ForagerOne alternative" | Forces a workflow comparison you lose |
| predicted setting (with confidence) | "wet lab" as fact | The model is ~92% accurate, not 100%; hybrids are weakest |
| built from public records | "scraped" | Implies unauthorized collection |
| [University] Lab Atlas / "Lab Atlas" | "Emory Lab Atlas" (externally) | Emory name-use policy |
| placement, took undergrads recently | "ranking" of faculty | Faculty-relations risk |

## Social Proof Guidelines
- **Only measured claims.** Emory pilot numbers once collected (students using, labs contacted, reply rates, placements), with dates.
- Quotes from the Pathways Center and faculty **with written permission**, attributed by role unless named consent is given.
- Faculty endorsements matter as much as office endorsements. Recruit 3-5 PIs as "faculty advisors" to the product.
- Never claim equity outcomes before the pilot measures them.

## Freemium Positioning
Not applicable (institution-paid). A free **preview atlas** for prospects is the equivalent "try before you buy".

## Red Flags
- Using "Emory" in commercial messaging before OTT and Communications approval.

## Yellow Flags
- "Every lab" must be scoped honestly (e.g., "every investigator in the School of Medicine, Winship, Rollins and College STEM").

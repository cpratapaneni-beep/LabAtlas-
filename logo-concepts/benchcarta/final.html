Benchcarta

A globe holding three electron orbits set 60° apart around a nucleus, inside a compass bezel. Three electrons, in coral, purple and blue, ride the orbits at matching points, 120° apart, and the nucleus is the three dots of the original Lab Atlas logo, clustered like protons and neutrons. The three orbits make the atom fully symmetrical; the bezel's ticks every 15° and its north pointer make the globe a compass. It began as the Open Globe, was turned to face up and closed, and now carries a third orbit on its vertical axis.

Construction

GlobeCircle, radius 50, stroke 7
OrbitsThree ellipses, 45 × 15, at 30°, 90° and 150° (60° apart), stroke 2.8
NucleusThe original logo's three dots clustered in a tight triangle: radius 5, each 5.6 from the centre, so they overlap slightly, with a fine outline in the ground colour between them. Purple at the top, coral lower left, blue lower right, echoing the electrons. The cluster fits inside every orbit.
ElectronsRadius 5.2, one on each orbit at the same point (40° along from the orbit's tip), so they keep 120° rotational symmetry: purple #A98AD4 at the top, coral #CF5F49 lower left, blue #3B8CCC lower right. On dark grounds the lighter tints are used (#C9A2F2, #E0705A, #5FA8DE). A 2.2 gap separates each from its orbit.
Bezel23 ticks every 15° from radius 58: 6 long at the quarters, 3 short between, stroke 2.2. A solid pointer replaces the tick at north.

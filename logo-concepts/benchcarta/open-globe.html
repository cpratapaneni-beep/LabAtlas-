Open Globe, turned

The Open Globe turned 90 degrees to the left, so the opening and its point face the top. With the opening facing up, the globe becomes a round-bottom flask, so the science is in the shape itself. Each version below adds a second reference at the point where the line leaves the globe: a flask neck, rising bubbles, a molecule, or an atom.

01

Flask Globe

Turned so the opening faces up, the globe becomes a round-bottom flask. Two straight walls rise from the opening as its neck and end at a lip. Inside, the latitude and the axis still make it a globe. The axis runs on up out of the neck to a point, so the lab and the map stay one shape.

02

Rising Bubbles

Turned upward, with the axis leaving the opening as a reaction does: a line rising into three bubbles that get bigger as they go. The globe reads as a flask on the bench, and the bubbles as something being discovered. The ellipse inside is the liquid's surface and the equator at once.

03

Molecule

Turned upward, the axis rises out of the opening and ends in a hexagon, the benzene ring every chemistry student learns to draw. The point at the end of the line, where the map leads, becomes a molecule.

Atom Globe

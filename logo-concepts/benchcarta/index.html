Benchcarta

Bench, where the research happens, and carta, the Latin and Italian word for map: a map of the bench. These seven marks are built the way car badges are, only circles, ellipses and straight lines at one weight. Each is shown flat, stamped in chrome, as its construction drawing, and next to the name.

Only primitives

Every line is part of a circle, an ellipse or a straight rule, and nothing is freehand.

One weight

Each mark holds a single main stroke weight, with a lighter one only for globe lines.

B, C, bench, map

Every mark is built from the name: the B, the C, the bench, the map, or two of them at once.

Badge-ready

Each one works flat, embossed or stamped in metal, and at 16 pixels.

01

Hemisphere B

The B's two bowls are the two hemispheres of an old world map, stacked on a straight stem. The upper globe is smaller and sits tangent on the lower one, as a B's bowls do. Each has one meridian and one parallel. It's the clearest link between the letter and the map in the name.

02

Linked bc

A lowercase b and c drawn as two equal rings and chained together like Audi's: the c passes over the b at the top and under it at the bottom. The bench and the map, linked. The c's opening faces east.

03

Bench Globe

A globe resting on a lab bench, which is the name in one picture: the bench, and the map of the world it opens onto. The bench is a straight top on two legs, and the globe touches it at a single point.

04

Compass C

An open C with a compass needle standing inside it, pointing north. The north half is solid and the south half is an outline, as on a mariner's compass. The C opens to the east.

Contour C

Three nested C's read as the contour lines of a survey map around a single summit, the point of the C. It's the simplest map symbol there is, made into the initial.

Pin C

A map pin with an open C inside its head: the place on the map where your bench is. It's the most literal mark, and the one that's easiest to understand at a glance.

Open Globe
